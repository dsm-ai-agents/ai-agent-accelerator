# India Establishment Registrations and Regulatory Licence Management Agreement

> **Synthetic demo document.** The entities, people, premises, registrations, deadlines and fees below are fictitious and intended only for demonstration of retrieval systems. This is not legal advice.

This Agreement is made on 1 June 2026 between **Banyan Regulatory Solutions Private Limited**, Chennai (“Provider”), and **Solstice Renewables India Private Limited**, Chennai (“Client”), the fictitious Indian GCC of a Danish renewable-energy business.

## 1. Engagement

The Client plans to occupy an office at 12 Marina Technology Estate, Chennai, with up to 320 employees performing engineering, procurement analytics and shared services. The Provider will create and administer an establishment-compliance register for the premises and workforce.

## 2. Initial registration matrix

Within fifteen business days after receiving required facts, the Provider will issue a matrix identifying registrations reasonably applicable to the disclosed operations, responsible owner, submission date, renewal date, evidence and status. The baseline scope covers:

- Tamil Nadu Shops and Establishments registration or intimation;
- professional tax employer registration;
- Employees’ Provident Fund and Employees’ State Insurance coordination, if applicable;
- labour welfare fund registration and calendar;
- contract labour principal-employer support where statutory thresholds are met;
- local trade or signage permissions identified for the premises;
- fire and building-compliance evidence tracking from the landlord; and
- internal display, notice and recordkeeping requirements.

The matrix is a practical compliance tool, not a formal legal opinion. Applicability dependent on disputed facts will be referred to Client counsel.

## 3. Filing and renewal management

The Provider will prepare routine applications from Client-approved data, coordinate signatures, submit filings where portal authority is granted, track acknowledgements, maintain electronic evidence, and issue renewal reminders ninety, sixty and thirty days before known expiry.

The Provider will conduct a quarterly desk review and one annual onsite records review. Findings will be rated Critical, High, Medium or Low. A Critical finding—one presenting a credible risk of closure, prosecution or immediate worker harm—will be escalated within four hours of confirmation.

## 4. Government interactions

The Provider may attend routine meetings with authorities and respond to administrative clarification requests. It will not agree to liability, admit a violation, pay a penalty, or make a representation outside Client-approved facts without written authority.

Unofficial payments and facilitation payments are prohibited. Any such request will be documented and escalated to the Client Compliance Officer. Government inspections, show-cause notices, prosecutions and appeals require a separately approved scope with counsel.

## 5. Client responsibilities

The Client shall:

1. provide accurate headcount, salary, shift, contractor and premises information;
2. supply leases, occupancy certificates, landlord no-objection documents and facility approvals;
3. execute forms and board authorities on time;
4. maintain workplace conditions and employee records;
5. implement remediation decisions and pay statutory contributions, fees and penalties;
6. notify the Provider before changing premises, adding shifts, engaging material contractor populations or commencing a regulated activity; and
7. give the Provider safe, reasonable site access.

The Provider may rely on landlord certificates and Client records unless clearly inconsistent. It is not responsible for the landlord’s building compliance or a contractor’s employment obligations.

## 6. Service levels and dependencies

Routine complete filings will be submitted at least five business days before the applicable deadline where complete inputs and signatures are received fifteen business days before that deadline. The Provider will acknowledge Client queries within one business day and provide a substantive response or action plan within three business days.

If Client information is late, the Provider will notify the compliance owner of the jeopardised deadline. Due dates are not warranties where a governmental portal is unavailable or an authority requests additional information.

## 7. Scope exclusions

Excluded matters include environmental permits for industrial operations, telecom licences, export-control approvals, special economic zone approvals, immigration, food licences, construction permits, electrical-inspector certification, litigation, employee claims and specialist health-and-safety engineering. The parties may add locations or registrations through written change control.

## 8. Fees and expenses

The fee for the twenty-four-month Term is **INR 3,120,000**, exclusive of tax, invoiced at INR 390,000 quarterly in advance. It includes the initial matrix, routine applications, calendar management, quarterly reviews and annual onsite review.

Government fees, stamp charges, laboratory or engineering certificates, counsel fees and travel outside Chennai are extra. The Provider must obtain approval for an individual third-party cost above INR 30,000. Invoices are payable within thirty days.

An additional office is charged at INR 420,000 annually after approval of a change order. A material change in headcount above 400 or commencement of round-the-clock operations may trigger a scope review.

## 9. Reporting and governance

The parties will hold monthly compliance calls and quarterly executive reviews. The Provider’s dashboard will show registrations, renewals, open evidence, regulatory changes, inspection activity and remediation owners.

The Provider will circulate a written regulatory-change alert within ten business days after identifying a published change reasonably relevant to the scope. It will state the expected effective date and proposed action but does not guarantee exhaustive monitoring of every local circular.

## 10. Records and audit trail

The Provider will retain submission receipts, approved forms, licences, correspondence and dashboards in the Client portal. Each record will identify its source and version. The Client may audit service records once annually on ten business days’ notice, subject to confidentiality and no access to other clients’ data.

Records will be retained for seven years after creation unless law or a Client hold requires longer retention.

## 11. Confidentiality and data protection

Each party shall protect confidential business, worker and premises information. The Provider will apply role-based access, encryption in transit and at rest, and annual security training. It will report a confirmed breach affecting Client personal data within forty-eight hours.

No personal data may be processed outside India without Client consent. Approved professional advisers may receive limited information where necessary and bound by confidentiality.

## 12. Compliance warranties

The Provider warrants that assigned personnel will have appropriate regulatory experience and that services will be performed with reasonable skill and care. Both parties will comply with applicable anti-bribery, sanctions and data-protection requirements.

The Provider does not warrant the grant or renewal of any registration. Regulatory interpretations and government processing times may change.

## 13. Liability and remediation

Neither party is liable for indirect or consequential damages. The Provider’s aggregate liability is capped at the fees paid in the twelve months before the event. For a governmental late fee caused solely by the Provider’s failure to submit an approved complete filing by a known deadline, the Provider will reimburse that late fee within the cap.

The cap does not apply to fraud, wilful misconduct or deliberate confidentiality breach. The Client is responsible for substantive noncompliance, unsafe premises, unpaid contributions, false information and failure to implement notified remediation.

## 14. Term and termination

The Term ends on 31 May 2028. Either party may terminate for material breach not cured within fifteen days. The Client may terminate for convenience after the first twelve months on sixty days’ notice. The Provider may suspend an affected service where it reasonably believes a filing would be false or unlawful.

At exit, the Provider will deliver an updated register, credentials held for the Client, open-item list and document export within twenty business days after payment of undisputed fees.

## 15. Disputes and general

Disputes will be escalated to senior management for twenty days, then resolved by one-arbitrator proceedings seated in Chennai and conducted in English under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. Courts in Chennai retain jurisdiction for interim measures.

Assignment requires consent except to an affiliate assuming the obligations. Force majeure excuses affected delay. The parties are independent contractors. This Agreement and signed changes are the complete agreement. Electronic signatures are valid.
