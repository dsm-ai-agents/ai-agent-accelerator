# GCC Transition and Exit Management Agreement

> **Synthetic demo document.** This document and every party, person, address, amount, asset, and event described in it are fictitious. It is intended only for RAG and contract-retrieval demonstrations, is not legal advice, and is not for signature.

This Agreement is made on 1 August 2026 between **VelaNova Energy S.A.**, a fictitious Spanish renewable-energy company (**Client**), and **HarborPath Transition Services Private Limited**, a fictitious Indian company at 12 Golf Course Extension Road, Gurugram 122018 (**Transition Manager**).

The Client is ending a build-operate arrangement for its 310-person Gurugram GCC and will transfer operational control from the incumbent operator to VelaNova Energy India Private Limited and selected successor vendors.

## 1. Objective and term

The Transition Manager shall plan, coordinate, evidence, and govern an orderly transfer of people, processes, data, assets, access, contracts, and operating knowledge.

The Agreement begins on 1 August 2026 and ends on 31 January 2027 unless the Client accepts transition completion earlier or extends the term by change order. There is no automatic renewal.

## 2. Transition principles

The parties shall prioritize:

1. employee welfare and clear communication;
2. uninterrupted critical operations;
3. lawful and secure movement of data;
4. verified transfer of knowledge and assets;
5. explicit ownership of risks and decisions;
6. separation of preparation, validation, and acceptance; and
7. timely revocation of obsolete access.

The Transition Manager coordinates decisions but does not become the employer, asset owner, contracting party, data controller, or final acceptance authority.

## 3. Workstreams

The Transition Manager shall manage the following workstreams:

- program governance and integrated planning;
- HR and employee transfer;
- process and knowledge transfer;
- legal, contracts, licences, and vendor novation;
- finance opening balances and outstanding items;
- technology, identity, application, and data migration;
- workplace assets, inventory, and facilities;
- communications and change management;
- continuity, parallel running, and cutover; and
- closure evidence and post-transition stabilization.

Dates depend on timely cooperation from the Client, incumbent operator, employees, landlords, authorities, banks, and successor providers.

## 5. Governance

A Transition Management Office (**TMO**) led by the Transition Manager shall maintain the master plan, dependency map, RAID log, decision log, deliverable register, communications calendar, and acceptance tracker.

Workstream leads meet twice weekly. The TMO meets weekly. A steering committee chaired by the Client’s Global Operations Director meets every two weeks and decides high-risk issues, scope changes, cutover, and acceptance.

Red issues affecting payroll, employee transfer, security, statutory compliance, finance control, or critical operations must be escalated within one hour. The Transition Manager may recommend delaying cutover; only the Client may decide.

## 6. Transition inventory

The Transition Manager shall coordinate a verified inventory of:

- 310 employee and 46 contractor roles;
- 72 documented business processes;
- 34 applications and 11 technical integrations;
- 418 laptops and 63 other tagged assets;
- 58 active vendor contracts;
- 26 recurring compliance obligations;
- operational and financial records;
- shared mailboxes, service accounts, certificates, and privileged credentials; and
- open incidents, audit findings, disputes, purchase orders, and receivables.

The inventory shall identify owner, custodian, transfer method, acceptance evidence, retention rule, dependency, and status. Missing or disputed items shall remain on the exception register and do not become accepted by silence.

## 7. Employee transition

The Client retains responsibility for the employment model, offers, compensation, consultation, statutory notices, immigration, benefits, and any termination. The Transition Manager shall coordinate data collection, employee communications, offer tracking, consent status, onboarding appointments, and issue escalation.

No individual employment data may be disclosed to a successor employer until a lawful basis, approved communication, and need-to-know access are confirmed.

The Transition Manager shall maintain an anonymized readiness dashboard showing eligible population, offers issued, acceptances, declines, background checks, payroll readiness, benefit enrollment, and unresolved cases. It shall not make hiring or termination decisions.

## 8. Knowledge transfer

For each process, the incumbent shall provide a process map, desktop procedure, calendar, control description, system guide, contact list, volume profile, KPI history, open items, known issues, and business-continuity steps.

Knowledge transfer shall use teach, observe, perform, and validate stages:

1. the incumbent explains and demonstrates;
2. the recipient shadows live work;
3. the recipient performs under supervision; and
4. the process owner validates independent performance.

Completion requires attendance records, materials delivered, proficiency assessment, open questions, and process-owner sign-off. A score below 80% requires remediation and reassessment.

## 9. Technology and data transition

The Transition Manager shall coordinate identity creation, role mapping, device readiness, application access, data extraction, secure transfer, reconciliation, cutover, and legacy-access revocation.

Production data shall move only through approved encrypted channels. Checksums, record counts, control totals, and exception reports shall evidence completeness. Personal data must be minimized. Test data must be masked or synthetic.

Privileged legacy access shall be revoked within two hours after confirmed cutover. Standard access shall be revoked by the end of the cutover day. Service accounts require named ownership and credential rotation.

The Transition Manager may not copy, host, or retain production data except in approved Client repositories.

## 10. Assets and workplace

Asset handover requires tag, description, serial number, assigned user or location, condition, ownership evidence, warranty status, and recipient acknowledgement. Unlocated assets must be investigated and reported; the Transition Manager is not financially responsible unless loss results from its custody or negligence.

Keys, access cards, badges, emergency contacts, maintenance logs, fit-out drawings, and facility approvals shall be handed over through signed registers. Hazardous, leased, or disputed assets must be separately identified.

## 11. Contracts and vendors

The Transition Manager shall classify each contract for novation, assignment, replacement, expiry, or termination; record consent requirements and notice dates; and coordinate documents with Client counsel.

It shall not provide legal advice or sign documents. A contract is treated as transferred only when required signatures and consents are confirmed. Supplier continuity plans are required for critical vendors lacking completed transfer ten Business Days before cutover.

## 12. Finance transition

The Transition Manager shall coordinate reconciliation of bank accounts, vendor and customer balances, accruals, prepaid items, fixed assets, employee expenses, open purchase orders, and outstanding invoices.

Opening-balance acceptance requires control totals and Client Finance approval. The Transition Manager shall not post entries, release payments, determine tax treatment, or certify financial statements.

## 13. Parallel run and cutover

Critical processes shall operate in parallel for at least one complete cycle, including one payroll, one accounts-payable payment cycle, and five Business Days of IT service desk operation.

Cutover criteria include:

- no unresolved critical security finding;
- payroll and statutory payment readiness;
- at least 95% of critical process knowledge-transfer sign-offs;
- all critical applications accessible and tested;
- reconciled opening balances or approved exceptions;
- successor staffing at agreed minimum levels;
- current continuity and rollback plans; and
- steering-committee approval.

The cutover runbook shall identify tasks in sequence, owners, timing, validation, rollback trigger, and communications.

## 14. Stabilization

For 20 Business Days after cutover, the Transition Manager shall run daily command-center calls, track incidents, coordinate incumbent support, and issue daily status reports for the first ten days and twice-weekly reports thereafter.

A Severity 1 issue requires immediate bridge activation and 15-minute updates. Root-cause analysis is due within five Business Days after resolution.

## 15. Acceptance

The Client accepts a workstream when required deliverables are complete, material exceptions have approved owners and dates, access and assets are reconciled, and the Client workstream owner signs an acceptance certificate.

Final acceptance requires all workstreams accepted, no open Severity 1 issue, no overdue critical action, final repositories delivered, data-retention actions agreed, and a closure report approved by the steering committee.

Acceptance does not waive latent defects, fraud, confidentiality breach, or obligations expressly surviving completion.

## 17. Client and third-party dependencies

The Client shall provide authority, decisions, repositories, approvers, legal and tax advice, system access, employee communications, and cooperation from the incumbent and successors. The Transition Manager shall identify dependency risk but cannot compel a third party.

If a dependency threatens a milestone, the Transition Manager shall provide impact, options, recommendation, and latest safe decision date.

The Client may request removal for substantiated misconduct, conflict, security risk, or persistent underperformance. Replacement transition is at the Transition Manager’s cost.

## 20. Confidentiality and privacy

Each party shall protect non-public business, employee, technical, financial, and transition information and use it only for the transition. Disclosure is limited to approved need-to-know recipients with equivalent duties.

The Transition Manager processes personal data only on documented instructions, applies least privilege and encryption, and notifies suspected incidents within two hours. It shall not use Client Data to train an AI model.

Confidentiality survives seven years; trade-secret obligations continue while protected.

## 21. Security

The Transition Manager shall use managed devices, multifactor authentication, encrypted storage and transfer, malware protection, access logging, quarterly access review, and secure deletion.

Transition repositories shall be hosted only in Client-approved locations. Downloads must be restricted. Records temporarily held by the Transition Manager shall be deleted within 30 days after acceptance, subject to legal retention.

## 22. Intellectual property

The Client owns Client Data and, upon payment, Client-specific transition plans, inventories, runbooks, reports, and knowledge materials created under this Agreement. The Transition Manager retains pre-existing methods, templates, and know-how and grants the Client a perpetual internal-use license where embedded.

Materials supplied by the incumbent remain subject to the incumbent’s rights; the Transition Manager gives no warranty of title to them.

## 23. Warranties and compliance

The Transition Manager warrants professional care, suitably qualified personnel, accurate reporting based on available evidence, and compliance with Applicable Law. It does not warrant employee acceptance, regulatory consent, third-party cooperation, or uninterrupted service where dependencies are outside its control.

Each party shall comply with anti-bribery, sanctions, privacy, employment, and competition laws. Conflicts must be disclosed.

## 24. Indemnities and liability

The Transition Manager indemnifies the Client for third-party claims arising from its infringement, unlawful data processing, or employment obligations to its personnel. The Client indemnifies the Transition Manager for claims arising from Client employment decisions, Client-provided materials, or unlawful instructions.

Neither party is liable for indirect or consequential loss. Aggregate liability is limited to the total fees under this Agreement, doubled for confidentiality, privacy, security, or IP indemnity. Fraud, wilful misconduct, and liabilities incapable of lawful limitation are uncapped.

## 25. Termination

Either party may terminate for material breach not cured within 15 Business Days or insolvency. The Client may terminate immediately for deliberate data misuse, bribery, or abandonment of the transition.

The Client may terminate for convenience on 30 days’ notice, paying completed milestones, work properly performed, and approved non-cancellable costs. The Transition Manager shall immediately preserve and hand over all transition records.

## 26. Disputes and governing law

Indian law governs. Senior executives shall attempt resolution for ten Business Days. Unresolved disputes shall be finally resolved by one arbitrator seated in New Delhi, in English, under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. Courts in New Delhi have jurisdiction for interim relief.

## 27. General

The Transition Manager is an independent contractor and has no authority to bind the Client. Assignment requires consent except to an Affiliate or business successor assuming the obligations. No publicity or use of names is permitted without approval.

Force majeure excuses affected delay where the party gives prompt notice, mitigates, and invokes continuity measures. It does not excuse confidentiality, data protection, payment, or handover of records already created.

This Agreement is the entire agreement for the transition. Amendments and waivers must be written and signed. If a provision is invalid, the remainder continues.
