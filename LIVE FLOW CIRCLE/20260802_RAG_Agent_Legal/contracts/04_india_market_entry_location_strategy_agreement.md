# India Market Entry and GCC Location Strategy Agreement

> **Synthetic demo document.** This document is fictional, is not intended for execution, and is supplied solely as a demonstration record.

This Agreement is made on 6 April 2026 between **Kestrel Bridge Market Entry Advisors Private Limited**, New Delhi (**Advisor**), and **Orchard Lantern Robotics Pte. Ltd.**, Singapore (**Client**).

## 1. Context

The Client develops warehouse robotics and fleet-orchestration software and is considering an Indian engineering and support center. The contemplated center will begin with approximately 65 employees and may expand to 220 within four years.

The Client appoints the Advisor to assess market entry and compare Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, and Gurugram. The engagement supports a location decision; it does not include incorporation, site brokerage, hiring, lease negotiation, or implementation.

## 2. Decision questions

The assessment will address:

- which city best supports robotics software, embedded systems, data engineering, technical support, and finance roles;
- likely compensation, benefits, recruitment lead time, and attrition;
- office availability and indicative occupancy costs;
- connectivity to Singapore and major Client markets;
- regulatory and operating considerations;
- ecosystem, university, supplier, and leadership depth;
- resilience, climate, infrastructure, and concentration risks; and
- potentially relevant state incentives.

The final recommendation will name one preferred city and one contingency city, with conditions that could change the ranking.

## 3. Services

### 3.1 Requirements and weighting

The Advisor will facilitate a workshop to confirm target functions, skill mix, ramp, facility needs, cost constraints, security considerations, and evaluation weights. The approved weighting becomes the baseline for scoring.

### 3.2 Talent assessment

The Advisor will use reputable published sources, anonymized market observations, and up to fifteen interviews with recruiters, employers, or specialists. It will estimate talent-pool depth, compensation ranges, hiring competition, attrition, notice periods, and leadership availability.

Compensation outputs are market estimates, not salary guarantees. Individual candidates, niche skills, and rapid hiring may fall outside estimated ranges.

### 3.3 Real-estate and infrastructure assessment

For each city, the Advisor will assess major business districts, Grade A office supply, managed workspace, indicative rent, common-area charges, fit-out ranges, commute patterns, airport access, utilities, and telecom resilience.

The Advisor may obtain non-binding indications from workspace providers and brokers. It does not verify title, negotiate leases, inspect structures, or provide architecture, engineering, environmental, or property-law advice.

### 3.4 Regulatory and incentive scan

The Advisor will provide a high-level summary of common establishment steps and publicly available state programs potentially relevant to technology, research, employment, or investment.

The Advisor does not promise eligibility, approval, quantum, or timing of an incentive. The Client must obtain legal and tax advice and engage appropriate authorities before relying on any program.

### 3.5 Cost model and recommendation

The Advisor will prepare a four-year model reflecting headcount ramp, compensation, benefits, office, technology, recruitment, travel, professional fees, and contingency. Assumptions will be documented, with sensitivity for inflation, attrition, and exchange rates.

The scorecard will combine quantitative and qualitative criteria. The Advisor will explain judgment calls and provide a recommendation workshop for the Client’s executive committee.

## 4. Deliverables

1. **Requirements memorandum**, due 20 April 2026.
2. **Five-city fact pack**, due 29 May 2026.
3. **Talent and compensation benchmark**, due 12 June 2026.
4. **Real-estate and operating-cost model**, due 26 June 2026.
5. **Weighted location scorecard**, due 10 July 2026.
6. **Final recommendation and executive presentation**, due 24 July 2026.
7. **Decision follow-up memorandum**, due within ten business days after the executive presentation.

The Advisor will provide editable spreadsheet models and PDF presentations. Raw licensed third-party datasets are excluded where redistribution is prohibited.

## 5. Acceptance

The Client will review each deliverable within seven business days and provide one consolidated set of comments. A deliverable is accepted when confirmed, used in a formal decision, or not rejected for a material failure to meet the agreed scope within the review period.

The Advisor will address one reasonable comment cycle. New cities, functions, scenarios, or material changes after baseline require change control.

## 6. Methodology and limitations

The Advisor will identify sources and the date of data where practicable. Market data changes, and the Client should refresh material assumptions before committing to a lease or hiring plan.

Interview statements may be anonymized. The Advisor will not disclose another client’s confidential information. Estimated talent pools may overlap across skills and should not be added without adjustment.

The Client remains responsible for the location and investment decision. The Advisor is not a fiduciary, investment adviser, real-estate broker, advocate before government, or guarantor of business results.

## 7. Client contributions

The Client will provide target job families, levels, hiring ramp, technology requirements, security classification, workstyle assumptions, travel patterns, budget parameters, and decision criteria. It will make stakeholders available for workshops and provide feedback within the timetable.

If the Client delays inputs by more than five business days, dates will be equitably extended. The Advisor will inform the Client of the estimated impact.
