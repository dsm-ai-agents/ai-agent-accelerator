# Procurement and Vendor Management Agreement

> **Synthetic demo document.** This is a fictional agreement prepared exclusively for demonstration, testing, indexing, and retrieval. It creates no rights or obligations and is not legal or procurement advice.

This Agreement is made on 1 July 2026 between **HelioWave Communications Pte. Ltd.**, a fictitious Singapore company (**Client**), and **SourceBridge GCC Services Private Limited**, a fictitious Indian company with offices at 7 Knowledge City Road, Hyderabad 500081 (**Provider**).

The Client’s Hyderabad GCC requires a transparent procurement intake, sourcing, onboarding, purchase administration, and supplier-governance function.

## 1. Appointment and term

The Client appoints the Provider on a non-exclusive basis for 24 months. The Agreement renews for 12 months unless either party gives 90 days’ notice before expiry.

## 2. Services

The Provider shall:

- operate a procurement intake desk;
- validate business requirements, budgets, approval codes, and sourcing routes;
- conduct requests for information, quotation, and proposal;
- support commercial evaluation and negotiation;
- perform vendor onboarding and due-diligence coordination;
- prepare purchase requisitions and purchase orders in Client systems;
- maintain the vendor, contract, and renewal registers;
- monitor supplier performance and remediation actions;
- coordinate vendor offboarding; and
- provide spend, savings, compliance, and cycle-time reports.

The Provider supports indirect procurement for IT hardware, software subscriptions, facilities, professional services, learning, recruitment, travel, and office supplies. Direct telecom network equipment, real-estate acquisition, insurance placement, litigation counsel, and mergers and acquisitions are excluded.

## 3. Authority boundaries

The Provider acts only as an administrator and adviser. It may not commit Client funds, select a vendor as final decision-maker, sign or amend a contract, waive a requirement, approve an invoice, authorize a payment, or communicate a binding award without written Client authority.

Only approvers named in the Client delegation matrix may approve spend. The Provider shall reject or return incomplete requests and maintain an auditable record of approvals.

## 4. Sourcing thresholds

Unless an exception is approved:

| Total commitment | Required process |
|---|---|
| Up to INR 250,000 | One documented market quotation |
| INR 250,001–1,500,000 | At least three written quotations |
| INR 1,500,001–10,000,000 | Formal competitive request with weighted evaluation |
| Above INR 10,000,000 | Formal request plus Procurement Council approval |

Requirements may not be split to avoid a threshold. Sole-source awards require a business justification, conflict declaration, price-reasonableness record, and approval one level above the normal delegation.

## 5. Vendor due diligence

Before onboarding, the Provider shall coordinate verification of legal identity, tax registration, bank details, beneficial ownership, sanctions and adverse media, conflicts, financial viability where material, information security where data access is involved, and acceptance of the Client supplier code.

High-risk vendors include those with privileged system access, personal-data processing, government interaction, critical operations, or annual spend above INR 10,000,000. High-risk vendors require annual reassessment; standard vendors require reassessment every two years.

The Provider shall escalate unresolved red flags and shall not activate a vendor without Client approval. Due diligence reduces but does not eliminate supplier risk and is not a guarantee of performance.

## 6. Competitive process integrity

Bid information shall be restricted to the evaluation team. The Provider shall use comparable commercial assumptions, record clarification questions, retain scoring evidence, and prohibit bidders from changing closed bids except through a documented best-and-final-offer process.

No Provider employee may accept gifts exceeding INR 2,500, cash, employment offers, commissions, or personal benefits from a prospective or active Client vendor. Actual or potential conflicts must be declared immediately. The Client may require replacement of conflicted personnel.

## 7. Contract and renewal management

The Provider shall maintain, for each covered contract, the owner, vendor, effective date, expiry, notice deadline, value, renewal mechanism, data-access status, and key obligations.

Renewal notices shall be issued to the business owner 120, 90, 60, and 30 days before the contractual notice deadline. The Provider is not responsible for an unintended renewal if the register lacked complete source documents and it reported that dependency.

The Provider shall support amendments and renewals but legal drafting and final legal approval remain with Client counsel.

## 8. Purchase-order administration

The Provider shall create purchase requisitions and draft purchase orders only after confirming approval, vendor status, commercial terms, tax coding supplied by the Client, and contract reference. Retrospective purchase orders require exception approval.

The Provider shall not confirm receipt of goods or services. The requesting business owner remains responsible for receipt confirmation and invoice acceptance.

## 9. Service Levels

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Intake acknowledgement | Within 4 business hours |
| Complete standard requisition converted to PO | Within 2 Business Days |
| Standard vendor onboarding | Within 5 Business Days |
| High-risk vendor onboarding | Within 10 Business Days |
| Renewal alerts | 100% by scheduled alert dates |
| Monthly spend dashboard | By Business Day 7 |
| Procurement record completeness | At least 98.5% |

Time spent awaiting requester, vendor, legal, security, tax, or approver input is excluded if the dependency is logged.

For each critical miss, the Client receives a credit of 2% of the monthly fee, capped at 8%. Persistent failure means three misses of the same target in a rolling six-month period.

## 10. Savings

**Validated Savings** means a reduction against an approved baseline, normalized for volume, specification, term, currency, and market movement, and signed off by the Client Finance Partner. Avoided price increases and demand reductions shall be reported separately and are not Validated Savings.

The Provider shall not receive a percentage of savings. This avoids incentives to overstate baselines or select unsustainable vendors.

## 11. Client obligations

The Client shall provide budgets, delegation matrices, security and legal reviewers, accurate specifications, timely evaluation participation, contract copies, vendor performance feedback, and access to relevant systems. It shall decide vendor awards and retain responsibility for business need, supplier use, acceptance, and payment.

## 12. Charges

The fixed fee is INR 900,000 per month, exclusive of taxes, totaling INR 21,600,000 for the initial term. It includes 180 procurement requests, 45 new vendor onboardings, 60 purchase orders, and 20 active sourcing events per month.

Sustained volume exceeding a baseline by more than 15% for three months triggers good-faith capacity review. No additional fee applies until a change order is signed. Invoices are payable within 30 days.

Third-party screening databases specifically requested by the Client and approved travel are reimbursable at cost without markup.

## 13. Personnel and subcontracting

The Provider shall assign a Procurement Lead, Vendor Risk Manager, two Category Managers, and adequate analysts. Key-person changes require ten Business Days’ notice where practicable.

Subcontracted due-diligence checks require Client approval. The Provider remains responsible for subcontractors and shall ensure equivalent confidentiality, security, and audit obligations.

## 14. Data and security

The Provider shall use Client-approved procurement systems, role-based access, multifactor authentication, managed devices, and encrypted transfer. Bid materials, pricing, bank information, and due-diligence findings are confidential.

A suspected compromise or fraudulent bank-change request must be reported within one hour. The Provider shall preserve relevant records and cooperate with investigation.

Personal data shall be processed only for sourcing and vendor administration and retained according to the Client schedule.

## 15. Intellectual property

Client specifications, bids, scorecards, supplier contracts, and registers belong to the Client. The Provider retains pre-existing templates, category methods, and generic analytics. Upon payment, the Client owns bespoke deliverables and receives a perpetual internal-use license to embedded Provider tools.

## 16. Compliance and audit

The Provider shall comply with anti-bribery, sanctions, competition, privacy, and employment laws. It shall retain sourcing and approval records for seven years.

The Client may conduct one ordinary audit annually on ten Business Days’ notice and targeted audits following suspected fraud, conflict, or control failure. The Provider shall remediate substantiated findings within an agreed period.

## 17. Confidentiality

Each party shall protect non-public information and disclose it only to need-to-know personnel, advisers, and approved subcontractors. Permitted exceptions cover public, previously known, independently developed, lawfully received, and legally compelled information. Obligations survive five years, and trade-secret protection continues while applicable.

## 18. Warranties

The Provider warrants professional performance, accurate recordkeeping based on supplied information, and impartial application of approved procurement rules. It does not warrant that any supplier will perform, that the lowest price will be selected, or that reported savings will recur.

## 19. Indemnities and liability

The Provider indemnifies the Client for third-party claims caused by Provider bribery, wilful disclosure of bid information, infringement by Provider deliverables, or employment claims from Provider personnel. The Client indemnifies the Provider for claims caused by Client specifications, final vendor selections, or unlawful instructions.

Neither party is liable for indirect or consequential loss. Aggregate liability is capped at 12 months’ fees; the cap is doubled for confidentiality breach, data breach, anti-bribery breach, or IP indemnity. Fraud and wilful misconduct are uncapped.

## 20. Continuity

The Provider shall maintain a continuity plan and restore intake and urgent vendor-risk escalation within four hours. Procurement records shall be backed up daily. Annual tests and remediation summaries shall be shared with the Client.

## 21. Termination and exit

Either party may terminate for uncured material breach after 30 days or insolvency. The Client may terminate immediately for bribery, deliberate sourcing manipulation, or unauthorized commitment of Client funds. Convenience termination requires 60 days’ notice after month six.

For 90 days after notice, the Provider shall transfer registers, sourcing files, approvals, due-diligence evidence, open events, supplier issues, and renewal calendars. Sixty exit hours are included; additional work is billed at INR 4,500 per hour.

## 22. Governing law and disputes

Indian law governs. Executives shall attempt resolution for 15 Business Days. Unresolved disputes shall be finally resolved by a sole arbitrator seated in Hyderabad under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, in English. Hyderabad courts may grant interim relief.

## 23. General

The Provider is an independent contractor. Assignment requires consent except to a successor of substantially all relevant business. Neither party may publicize the relationship without approval. Force majeure excuses affected delay, subject to notice and mitigation, but not payment or confidentiality duties.

This Agreement is the complete agreement for its subject. Amendments, waivers, and change orders must be written and signed.
