# Office Lease and Fit-Out Coordination Agreement

> **Synthetic demo document.** All parties, properties, people, amounts and events are fictitious and created solely for a RAG demonstration. This is not legal advice and creates no real tenancy.

This Agreement is made on 20 July 2026 among Sahyadri Commercial Estates LLP (“Landlord”), Velorum Mobility Technologies India Private Limited (“Tenant”) and, solely for the coordination obligations in Clause 7, Meridian Harbor GCC Advisory Private Limited (“Fit-Out Coordinator”).

## 1. Leased premises

Landlord leases to Tenant approximately 45,000 rentable square feet on floors 10 and 11 of the fictitious Horizon Forge Tower, Kharadi, Pune, together with 45 car-parking spaces and proportionate use of common areas (“Premises”). Tenant shall use the Premises as a global engineering and digital operations centre, including laboratories for non-hazardous electronics testing. Manufacturing, retail use and storage of combustible material are prohibited.

The final rentable area shall be certified by an independent architect under the agreed measurement standard. If it differs by more than 2%, rent and common-area charges shall be adjusted prospectively; the lease term shall not change.

## 2. Term, possession and renewal

The lease term is five years beginning 1 September 2026. Landlord shall deliver vacant possession for fit-out access on 1 June 2026 (“Possession Date”). The period from Possession Date through 31 August 2026 is the “Fit-Out Period.”

Tenant has one option to renew for three years by giving nine months’ notice. Renewal rent shall be the lower of prevailing market rent established by two independent brokers or 115% of the final-year rent. Other provisions continue except incentives and the rent-free fit-out concession.

The first 36 months after rent commencement are a lock-in period. Tenant’s convenience exit during lock-in requires payment of rent for the remaining lock-in period, subject to Landlord’s duty to seek a replacement tenant.

## 3. Conditions at handover

At possession, Landlord shall provide a weather-tight shell; operational lifts; code-compliant fire stairs and sprinklers; sanctioned electrical load of 500 kVA; two diverse telecom entry routes; finished washrooms; chilled-water connection points; and clear floor plates free of prior occupants.

Tenant shall inspect within five business days. Failure affecting safe fit-out must be corrected within ten business days. Other deficiencies shall be placed on a snag list for correction within 20 business days. Fit-out access does not constitute acceptance of concealed structural defects.

## 4. Rent and escalation

Base rent is INR 2,700,000 per month plus taxes, beginning on 1 September 2026 or, if Landlord delays a required handover condition, the date the condition is completed. Tenant-caused delay does not postpone rent commencement.

Base rent increases by 15% after every 36 months. The stated contract value is the un-escalated base rent for 70 months including the fit-out period’s nominal access charge; it excludes taxes, parking, utilities and common-area maintenance.

Tenant shall also pay INR 337,500 per month as estimated common-area maintenance, reconciled annually against audited actual cost. Landlord shall provide the supporting cost statement within 120 days after each financial year.

Rent is payable monthly in advance by the seventh day. A good-faith dispute does not permit withholding more than the disputed amount.

## 5. Security deposit and parking

Tenant shall maintain an interest-free security deposit of INR 16,200,000, equal to six months’ initial base rent. Landlord may apply it only against overdue undisputed sums or documented damage beyond normal wear, after notice and ten business days to cure. Tenant shall replenish valid deductions within 15 days.

The deposit balance shall be returned within 60 days after vacant possession and reconciliation. Delay attracts interest at the Reserve Bank of India repo rate plus 2%.

Parking is charged at INR 5,000 per car space per month, escalating with base rent. Bicycle parking for 60 cycles is included.

## 6. Fit-out responsibility

Tenant funds and owns the internal fit-out, including partitions, workstations, acoustic treatment, supplemental cooling, internal electrical distribution, structured cabling, access control and branded signage. Landlord is responsible for base-building structure, façade, risers and central systems.

Tenant shall submit drawings for approval. Landlord must respond within seven business days; silence after a reminder and three further business days constitutes approval for non-structural works. Approval does not transfer design responsibility to Landlord.

Works shall be conducted by licensed contractors, comply with law and building rules, and maintain adequate insurance. No structural cutting, façade penetration or change to central fire systems is permitted without written engineering approval.

## 7. Fit-Out Coordinator

Meridian Harbor shall maintain the integrated fit-out schedule, coordinate design submissions, organise weekly site meetings, track decisions and risks, facilitate landlord and vendor access, and report forecast cost and completion.

Meridian Harbor does not warrant contractor workmanship, certify structural safety, act as architect of record, or approve expenditure for Tenant. Only Tenant may instruct a scope or budget change. Meridian Harbor shall notify Tenant within one business day of a forecast delay exceeding five working days or a forecast variance exceeding INR 1,000,000.

Tenant shall pay Meridian Harbor a fixed coordination fee of INR 4,800,000 under a separate invoice schedule. This fee is not rent and is excluded from the stated lease value.

## 8. Delays

If Landlord delays possession or a base-building dependency, rent commencement moves day-for-day. For delay beyond 30 days, Landlord shall reimburse documented contractor standby charges up to INR 3,000,000. For delay beyond 60 days, Tenant may terminate and receive the full deposit plus documented design costs capped at INR 5,000,000.

If Tenant’s contractors delay completion, rent remains payable from the scheduled commencement date. Force majeure extends affected milestones but does not excuse accrued payment obligations.

## 9. Utilities, maintenance and services

Tenant shall pay metered electricity, backup-power fuel allocation, water consumption above normal office use and telecom charges. Landlord shall maintain structure, lifts, central HVAC plant, common electrical systems, fire-life-safety systems, façade and common areas.

Tenant shall maintain internal equipment and promptly repair damage caused by its personnel. Landlord shall provide 48 hours’ notice for planned shutdowns and immediate notice of emergency shutdowns. No non-emergency maintenance affecting business shall occur during Tenant’s core hours without consent.

## 10. Alterations, assignment and subletting

After the initial fit-out, Tenant may perform non-structural alterations costing less than INR 2,500,000 after notice; other alterations require consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.

Tenant may assign the lease or sublet up to 30% of the Premises to an affiliate on notice, provided Tenant remains liable and use remains permitted. Any other transfer requires Landlord’s consent. A change in ultimate control of Tenant’s foreign parent is not an assignment.

## 11. Compliance and insurance

Landlord warrants that the building has required occupancy, fire and structural approvals on the Possession Date. Tenant is responsible for registrations arising from its business and workforce. Each party shall promptly provide documents reasonably needed for the other’s compliance.

Landlord shall maintain building replacement and public-liability cover. Tenant shall maintain fit-out/property cover, employee insurance and public-liability cover of at least INR 100,000,000.

## 12. Damage and casualty

If casualty makes more than 25% of the Premises unusable, rent and common-area charges abate proportionately. Landlord shall provide a restoration estimate within 20 business days. Either party may terminate if restoration is reasonably expected to exceed 180 days. Insurance proceeds relating to Tenant-owned fit-out belong to Tenant.

## 13. Default and termination

A payment default is curable within 15 business days after notice. Other material defaults are curable within 30 days, extended where cure reasonably requires longer and is diligently pursued. Immediate termination is permitted for insolvency, unlawful use or deliberate material damage.

Landlord shall not lock out Tenant, disconnect essential services or seize property except through lawful process. Tenant may terminate for persistent material service failure occurring three times in 90 days after notice and failed cure.

## 14. Exit and reinstatement

At expiry, Tenant shall remove movable equipment, confidential material and branded signage, repair removal damage and return access credentials. Tenant need not remove ordinary partitions, cabling trays, ceilings or landlord-approved improvements unless Landlord identified the item for removal when approving it.

The parties shall conduct an exit inspection 60 days before expiry. Landlord shall issue a specific reinstatement list within ten business days. Claims not included are waived except concealed damage. Tenant may elect to pay an agreed reinstatement sum instead of performing work.

## 15. Confidentiality, liability and disputes

Commercial terms, security plans and non-public building information are confidential for five years. Mandatory disclosures are permitted after notice where lawful.

No party is liable for indirect or consequential loss. Landlord’s and Tenant’s aggregate liability is capped at 12 months’ base rent, excluding fraud, wilful misconduct, personal injury, property damage, confidentiality breach and payment obligations. Meridian Harbor’s liability under this Agreement is capped at its fit-out coordination fee, with the same exceptions.

Disputes shall be escalated for 20 business days and then resolved by a sole arbitrator seated in Pune, in English, under Indian arbitration law. Pune courts have jurisdiction for interim measures.
