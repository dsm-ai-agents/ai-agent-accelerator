## Summary

RetailMax, an apparel and home goods retailer with 220 stores and a growing online channel, used NexaFlow to automate order exception handling, returns and refunds, and store maintenance requests. The project was scoped tightly and went live in 4 weeks, the fastest end of the standard 4–6 week enterprise timeline. RetailMax reduced order and refund errors by 30% and saves 18 hours per week in customer service operations.

### The Problem

RetailMax's online sales had grown 60% in two years, but back-office processes had not kept up.

- Order exceptions (address problems, payment holds, out-of-stock items) were handled from a shared Zendesk view with no prioritisation.
- Refunds for returns required agents to check Shopify, NetSuite and the warehouse system manually; about **1 in 20 refunds** had an error (wrong amount, duplicate refund or missed restocking).
- Store maintenance requests were emailed to regional managers and often lost.
- The customer service operations team spent around **18 hours per week** on manual reconciliation.
- Leadership wanted the solution live **before the holiday peak**, giving the team 5 weeks.

### The Implementation

RetailMax followed NexaFlow's standard nine-phase onboarding process and went live in **4 weeks**.

| Week | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Discovery and solution design (combined, 3 workflows in scope) |
| 1–2 | Environment setup with Google Workspace SSO and SCIM |
| 2 | Shopify Plus, NetSuite, Zendesk and Slack connectors configured |
| 2–3 | Migration of 35,000 open orders and return authorisations |
| 3 | UAT with customer service leads and 5 pilot stores |
| 4 | Operator training, 10-minute approver video for store managers, go-live |

Factors that kept it fast: four systems with pre-built connectors, a small migration, a standard security questionnaire completed in one week, and a full-time project lead on the RetailMax side.

### The Solution

**Key workflows automated:**

1. **Order exception triage** — an AI agent classifies each exception, fixes simple address issues automatically and routes the rest to the right queue by priority and order value.
2. **Returns and refund processing** — when a return is received, NexaFlow verifies the item, calculates the refund, issues it in Shopify, updates NetSuite and triggers restocking.
3. **Duplicate refund prevention** — a rule checks for existing refunds before processing.
4. **Store maintenance requests** — store managers submit requests via a form; approvals happen in Slack; vendors are dispatched automatically.
5. **Peak season dashboard** — Command Center tracks exception backlog and refund SLA in real time.

### Measurable Results

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Refund and order processing errors | 1 in 20 | 1 in 29 | **30% fewer errors** |
| Manual reconciliation effort | ~18 hours/week | Minimal | **18 hours saved per week** |
| Average refund processing time | 4.2 days | 1.5 days | 64% faster |
| Holiday peak exception backlog | 2,400 (prior year) | 600 | 75% lower |

### Customer Quote

"We had five weeks before peak season. NexaFlow was live in four, and it was the calmest holiday our service team has had." — Director of Customer Operations, RetailMax

### Lessons Learned

- A hard deadline plus a narrow scope (three workflows) made a 4-week go-live realistic.
- Giving store managers free Approver access in Slack avoided buying extra licenses.
- Phase 2 (vendor invoice automation) started after hypercare ended.
